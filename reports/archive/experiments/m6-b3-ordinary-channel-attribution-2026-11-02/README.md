# B3 f64 ordinary-channel attribution screen — 2026-11-02

## Scope and source

This diagnostic isolates the ordinary B3 f64 output-accumulation passes on GL T720, using source `85f74d6d91863278f06891205d22d30207e2649d` and DUCC 0.42.0 `42ce8ab5c5d79e0e2fd9af4a09c2ea4693520e1b`. The current `dev` tip is `fb3ace0f87fdf542ae26d43213f24ac48b13e65d`; its B3 Rust source files are byte-identical to the measured source. No production file was modified.

The active route is `FusedScalarAnalysisPair::with_simd` and `fused_analysis_two_pair_steps_with_simd` in `src/legendre/scalar_paired/analysis.rs`. The recurrence advances and accumulates the first channel in the two-degree SIMD loop; `AnalysisTwoBasisAccumulation` then accumulates the second channel from the saved basis vectors. The one-pair tail uses `AnalysisPairAccumulation`.

## Method

The diagnostic worktree was detached at `85f74d6d`. Three release binaries were built with `-C target-cpu=x86-64-v3 -Aunexpected_cfgs`, using Pulp V3 AVX2/FMA and native f64. The `both` binary executes production arithmetic. `first_only` compiles out the second channel accumulation and writes zeros to that channel. `recurrence_only` compiles out both coefficient channels while retaining recurrence advancement and writes zeros to outputs. These outputs are intentionally invalid. Repeat/reset consistency was checked only to ensure deterministic worker timing and workspace reuse; it is not correctness validation.

For full M720 and reduced M180 GL T720, q1 and q8 were pinned to the first one/eight physical CPUs on the AMD Ryzen 9 5950X. Three interleaved process pairs rotated variant order. Each worker collected ten warm batches, two transforms per batch, targeting 25 ms. Ratios and 95% intervals use paired log ratios and Student-t with df=2. `perf stat` is unavailable (`perf_event_paranoid=4`). The full raw capture remains in bounded local scratch; `paired-summary.json` preserves per-pair medians, ratios, fixture hashes, and binary hashes without machine-local file paths.

## Result

| Cell | First channel only / both | Recurrence only / first channel | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Full q1 | 0.837 [0.719, 0.922] | 0.956 [0.891, 1.022] | Omitting the second channel is faster; first-channel removal is unresolved. |
| Full q8 | 0.813 [0.619, 0.968] | 0.975 [0.884, 1.083] | Second-channel removal is faster; noisy q8 pair; first-channel removal is unresolved. |
| Reduced q1 | 0.757 [0.744, 0.774] | 0.969 [0.939, 0.993] | Second-channel removal is faster; first-channel removal is a small 3.1% effect. |
| Reduced q8 | 0.781 [0.741, 0.815] | 0.991 [0.928, 1.096] | Second-channel removal is faster; first-channel removal is unresolved. |

The first ratio says that removing the second-channel accumulation changes complete-call time by 16–24% in this screen. The second ratio says that removing first-channel coefficient accumulation on top of the recurrence-only version changes time by 0–4% by point estimate; only reduced q1 excludes parity. These are not additive operation costs: each compile-time skip changes code generation and register allocation. The second channel is required output work, also present in DUCC, so this is not a deletion opportunity.

The current B3 Rust/DUCC baseline remains full q1 1.109 [1.094, 1.120], full q8 1.139 [1.108, 1.164], reduced q1 1.095 [1.046, 1.151], reduced q8 1.130 [1.058, 1.185], from the separate exact-source 85f74d6d screen. That screen passed finite, repeat, and workspace-reset checks with maximum Rust/DUCC relative L2 `5.21e-14`. The skip variants are not compared to DUCC.

## Sampled timer follow-up — inconclusive

A diagnostic-only timer was added around the ordinary two-degree recurrence/first-channel helper, the second-channel accumulation helper, and the pair-state range scan. It samples orders with `m % 64 == 0` in the same full two-channel transform. The profiled and uninstrumented runs produced identical output checksums for every generated fixture/cell; this is repeatability evidence, not an independent accuracy check. The deterministic maps are generated by the archived example; their hashes and executable hashes are in `timer-probe/metadata.json`.

| Cell | Profiled / plain whole-call | Sampled recurrence + first | Sampled second channel | Sampled range scan | Groups / sampled calls |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Full q1 | 1.059 [1.022, 1.098] | 262.9 us | 220.8 us | 124.0 us | 6,124 / 59 |
| Full q8 | 1.139 [0.934, 1.389] | 282.0 us | 232.7 us | 129.5 us | 6,124 / 59 |
| Reduced q1 | 1.057 [1.010, 1.105] | 130.5 us | 109.0 us | 60.1 us | 2,952 / 18 |
| Reduced q8 | 0.992 [0.837, 1.175] | 132.2 us | 110.8 us | 60.4 us | 2,952 / 18 |

These stage values are **not reliable cost shares**. A tight calibration of the same `Instant::now`/`elapsed` pair measured 21.69 ns per timer pair. Three pairs per group contribute about 65 ns of overhead to approximately 99–105 ns of reported combined stage time per group, before accounting for changed code generation. The whole-call probe/plain ratio also shows 5–14% overhead in q1 and full q8, with wide q8 uncertainty. The profiler perturbs the very path it is meant to split. Treat these timings as inconclusive; they do not show that recurrence, second-channel accumulation, or range checking is the dominant avoidable cost.

The sampled-timer patch and raw 24-run screen are preserved under `timer-probe/`. No production source or performance claim changed. The follow-up current-source Callgrind attribution is complete and archived in [m6-b3-callgrind-attribution-2026-11-02](../m6-b3-callgrind-attribution-2026-11-02/README.md): it found expected recurrence and two-channel instruction work, but no redundant operation and no cycle attribution. `perf_event_paranoid=4` still blocks PMU access. Keep the production kernels unchanged; the next distinguishing measurement requires PMU-enabled cycle/front-end/backend data or equivalent cycle attribution.
