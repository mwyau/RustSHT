# Reusable f32 bridge T1023 scalar q1 screen protocol v1

## Question

Does retaining conversion buffers improve a complete f32 scalar synthesis
compared with the existing allocating f32-to-f64 bridge? This is a one-process
screen only. It cannot accept a production route.

## Frozen inputs

- RustSHT source: cleaned `opt/sht-performance-parity` commit
  `83bd755edfa44e6b60a19548025d316c20cc49fe`.
- Prototype sources:
  `m6_f32_reusable_bridge.rs` SHA-256
  `d612b5a0afe54926e27d43c2edbc0672e532dafc9383742bd36ce1010222cbd9`;
  `reusable_bridge.rs` SHA-256
  `91931b1f820197fec1f2d6b409e680566bab4eb7169d324c0a7c2015fb36d309`.
- DUCC comparer: `compare_ducc.py` SHA-256
  `f2a1eea20447c34b4ecb429cbc39e08618cfa5e0280beb7b84dac10fd44e098e`;
  it accepts legacy and reusable-prototype dump names, reports both bridge
  variants, and calculates the fixed CV and drift checks for DUCC.
- Workload: GL f32 scalar STANDARD synthesis, T1023, `1024x2048`, full
  `mmax=1023`, `phi0=0.37`, one worker. Coefficients are generated by the
  frozen example source and widened exactly for the f64 variants.
- Variants, measured in the same process: native f32, allocating f64 bridge,
  reusable-buffer f64 bridge, and native f64 with the same rounded inputs.
  Timed bridge calls include widening, the transform, and narrowing. Reusable
  plan, workspace, and conversion-buffer construction are reported separately.
- DUCC comparison: version and source revision recorded by the frozen
  `compare_ducc.py`; its q1 process uses the same map and one warm-up plus five
  timed calls. Numerical comparisons use the complete output arrays.

## Validity and decision rules

- Each Rust variant and DUCC series must have population CV at most 5% and
  first-three/final-two median drift at most 5%. Every sample is retained. An
  invalid series stops this screen; no retries or sample removal are allowed.
- Linux CPU PSI `some avg10` above 2.00% at preflight or postflight rejects the
  process. Readings from 0.50% through 2.00% are warnings and are reported
  exactly. Actual worker placement, frequency, involuntary context switches,
  competing CPU work, cgroup pressure, and clear interference are reviewed;
  clear interference makes the result inconclusive below the PSI limit.
- Reusable bridge output must be bitwise equal to the corresponding f64 result
  after f32 narrowing, and buffer capacities must remain unchanged. DUCC
  relative-L2 and maximum absolute errors are reported without changing the
  established numerical tolerances.
- Continue to a paired multi-process screen only if every timing and numerical
  validity check passes and the reusable bridge median is at least 3% lower
  than the allocating bridge median. This threshold selects further
  investigation; it is not a production acceptance threshold.

Build and all compilation finish before timing starts. Raw preflight,
postflight, process, frequency, affinity, GNU-time, and console captures remain
local and untracked. Commit only sanitized samples, aggregate RSS, validity
decisions, numerical errors, source/binary hashes, and conclusions.
