# P01 scalar synthesis paired screen

This screen compares the pre-P01 working-tree executable with the scalar-only transition candidate. The pre-P01 executable is the native `m1b_bench` used for the matched M6 current-source baseline; its hash and working-tree patch are retained as `baseline-binary.sha256` and `pre-p01-source.patch`. The candidate is built from the current working tree with Rust 1.99.0 and retained as `candidate-binary.sha256` and `candidate-source.patch`. Each fresh process uses `m1b_bench` with GL, STANDARD, f64, `phi0=0.37`, full `mmax=L`, `ntheta=L+1`, `nphi=2*(L+1)`, both whole-transform operations, and `requested-only`. The harness warms each operation and records five elapsed samples. q1 runs are pinned to one CPU core; q8 runs are 8 pinned CPU cores. `/usr/bin/time -v` records process wall time, CPU use, and peak RSS. The local preflight reviews CPU pressure and competing workloads before each process; only its validity decision is summarized in Git. Raw process records remain local and untracked. Process order alternates baseline-first and candidate-first across pairs. Pair ratios compare the candidate and baseline median of the five samples for each operation. The summary reports medians and deterministic 95% bootstrap percentile intervals over paired ratios, plus sample ranges and RSS changes. No unfavorable sample is omitted. The baseline reference campaign in `../m6-current-baseline/` supplies the pinned DUCC and SHTns context; these paired native processes isolate P01 against that same pre-P01 Rust source. The paired matrix contains six pairs per cell: scalar and spin 1 at T511/q1, scalar at T1023/q1 and T2047/q1, and scalar at T1023/q8 and T2047/q8. `process-pairs.csv` retains all five samples for each paired process and operation; `summary.csv` aggregates six paired ratios per operation. The bootstrap resamples the six pair ratios with replacement 50,000 times using Python `random.Random(20261017)` and linear percentile interpolation. RSS is from `/usr/bin/time -v`; operation-level timing uses the harness sample vectors. `summarize.py` regenerates the structured summaries from benchmark timing output and local GNU-time records; neither capture is committed. Binary SHA-256 values are in `baseline-binary.sha256` and `candidate-binary.sha256`. Their matching source snapshots are `pre-p01-source.patch` (SHA-256 `94faf9984c65013e4b05dbe12a69f2de2913bf2990ce6e97c235621cb31c374f`) and `candidate-source.patch` (SHA-256 `fff74a3059aec58741072b3eb1a96421c006ddcc4ba6847949098d17266faecf`). The benchmark records allowed hardware/software specifications and workload configuration. Raw process placement, host-pressure and preflight evidence remains local and untracked. No preflight guard refused a run. CPU-pressure validity was reviewed under the declared protocol; exact transient readings remain local. The T1023/q8 analysis pair-5 candidate has a retained high-sample cluster (25.350–26.234 ms versus the cell's usual roughly 19–22 ms); its preflight showed no competing process, so this is reported as unexplained variability rather than discarded or attributed to a confirmed external workload. The predeclared performance requirements are meaningful full-transform scalar synthesis improvement at T1023 and T2047, with no repeatable regression above 2% in the T511 scalar or spin-1 controls. q8 results are reported with their spread and are descriptive where process variability dominates.
